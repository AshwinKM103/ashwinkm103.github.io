The project addresses the lab’s need for a dependable, customizable digital-twin platform serving
multiple manufacturers and their customers. Its **product vision** connects turbine measurements
to equipment context, shared investigations, and accountable maintenance. Manufacturers inspect
their supplied fleets across customer sites; buyers oversee equipment across suppliers; operators
explore the turbines assigned to them. Equipment definitions, sensor-to-schematic mappings, staff
assignments, and explicit sharing permissions adapt the same foundation to different installations.

An operator opens a turbine, examines readings and historical trends, and checks an unusual
condition against operating state and sensor quality. The copilot brings together authorized
telemetry queries, charts, alarms, and cited procedures to explain the evidence and prepare shift
reports or reviewable maintenance proposals. Buyers route issues to internal maintenance or an
authorized supplier; engineers record findings and work for buyer review.

The architecture preserves asset identity, timestamps, units, source records, and freshness
alongside observations. Domain services supply numerical facts; the model organizes and explains
them. Missing or stale signals remain visible, diagnostic hypotheses remain distinct from
observations, and server-enforced permissions govern data, documents, and conversations. Replay
of recorded turbine telemetry supports reproducible investigation, while recoverable provisioning
and separate cloud delivery components support the platform design. The engineering contribution
is this connected workflow: physical measurements become traceable evidence that people can
inspect, question, and use in operational decisions.
